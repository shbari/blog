<h2>Why are mobile plans so expensive in Japan?</h2>

<p>
Many newcomers are surprised by the price of Japanese mobile phone plans.
The market has long been dominated by the "Big Three" carriers:
<strong>NTT Docomo</strong>, <strong>au</strong>, and <strong>SoftBank</strong>.
These companies provide excellent nationwide coverage, but their plans are
generally more expensive than many people expect.
</p>

<p>
A newer competitor, <strong>Rakuten Mobile</strong>, has been growing rapidly
by offering lower prices. While its network coverage is still smaller than the
Big Three, it has become an attractive option for budget-conscious users.
</p>

<p>
Another category worth knowing is <strong>MVNOs (Mobile Virtual Network Operators)</strong>.
These companies lease network access from the major carriers and can offer
much cheaper plans.
</p>

<div class="highlight">
<strong>Before choosing a SIM card, pay attention to what it includes.</strong>
Japanese SIM cards may be:
<ul>
<li>📶 Data only</li>
<li>💬 Data + SMS</li>
<li>📞 Data + SMS + Voice calls</li>
</ul>

Choosing the wrong type can become inconvenient later, so always check the plan carefully.
</div>

</div>

<h2>Final Thoughts</h2>

<p>
The "best" mobile plan depends on your budget and how much data you use.
If you don't have a credit card, carriers like <strong>Ahamo</strong> and
<strong>Rakuten Mobile</strong> offer excellent choices. If you do have a
credit card, MVNOs such as <strong>B-mobile</strong>, <strong>IIJmio</strong>,
<strong>mineo</strong>, and <strong>NURO Mobile</strong> can save you a
considerable amount every month.
</p>

<p>
Before signing up, always verify:
</p>

<ul>
<li>✔ Voice calls vs. data-only service</li>
<li>✔ Payment methods accepted</li>
<li>✔ Network coverage in your area</li>
<li>✔ Contract requirements and cancellation fees</li>
</ul>

<p>
With a little research, you can easily save thousands of yen each year without
sacrificing reliable service.
</p>
